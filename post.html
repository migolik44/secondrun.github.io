How to price a used iPhone in 2026: a practical ledger

Mint Aug 18, 2026 7 min read

Most sellers price by ear — a quick glance at a marketplace and a guess. The ones who consistently get paid fairly work from three numbers: base depreciation, grade, and battery health. Here's how to run that math on your own phone.

Start with base depreciation, not the sticker price

Every model has a rough depreciation curve independent of condition: roughly 25–30% in year one, then 12–15% per year after, until it flattens out around the four-year mark. That curve is your ceiling. Grade and battery health only ever pull the price down from there — they don't add to it.

Then apply the grade

Once you know the ceiling, your condition grade tells you how far below it you land. Use the grading guide honestly — buyers check, and an inflated grade costs you more in return requests than it gains you in asking price.

  • Mint: full ceiling price, sometimes a small premium for original box and accessories.
  • Good: 10–15% below ceiling. This is where most used iPhones actually sell.
  • Fair: 25–35% below ceiling, or more if the screen has been replaced with a non-original part.
  • Poor: parts value only — usually 15–20% of original launch price regardless of model.

Battery health is the one number buyers ask for before anything else. Below 80%, expect a direct, itemized deduction — not a vague "good condition" discount.

The two checks most sellers skip

Two things quietly tank a sale price after the fact, and both take under a minute to check before you list.

  1. Find My iPhone / activation lock. Turn it off before listing. A phone still tied to an iCloud account is worth a fraction of its price to a buyer, because they can't be sure it'll ever fully activate.
  2. Carrier lock status. A locked phone sells for 15–25% less than an unlocked one, even in the same market. If you can get it unlocked before selling, do that first — it usually pays for itself several times over.
Quick gut check: take your model's ceiling price, apply your honest grade deduction, then apply a battery deduction if it's under 85%. If your planned asking price is more than 5% above that number, you're pricing on hope, not the market.

Where this breaks down

Regional demand shifts this by a further 10% or so — storm-season regions see a short bump in rugged-case-friendly models, and a new launch always softens prices on the outgoing generation for about six weeks. Treat the numbers above as a starting point you adjust from, not a fixed table.
